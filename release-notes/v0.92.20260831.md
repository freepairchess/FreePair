# FreePair v0.92.20260831

Windows release. macOS and Linux remain on `v0.90.20260829`.

## What's new

### Printing

- **A quad now prints on one sheet.** A quad is four players and three
  rounds of two boards, so a page per round was three near-empty sheets —
  and a ten-quad scholastic was thirty of them. All three rounds now
  share a page: rounds 1 and 2 side by side, round 3 below round 1. This
  is the default, on both a section's own **Print** and the event-wide
  **All Pairings**.

- **Rounds already played stay on the sheet.** A quad's whole schedule is
  paired the moment you pair it, so the sheet is complete from the start
  and does not renumber itself as the day goes on.

- **Turn it off per-report if you want a page per round.** **Print the
  whole quad on one page** lives in **Page setup**, and appears there only
  when the print you are about to make actually covers a quad. Like the
  rest of page setup, the choice is remembered.

- **The quad sheet is always landscape**, even if you set the pairing
  sheet to portrait. Two tables side by side need the width; on portrait
  paper the type would shrink until the sheet was hard to read.

### Printing the right round

- **Pairings now print the round a section is actually on** — the first
  round that is not finished, or the last one once they all are.
  Previously an unasked-for pairings print took the last round on the
  schedule. That is the same round for a Swiss, but a quad or round robin
  is paired all at once, so a freshly paired quad handed you the round 3
  sheet before anyone had played round 1.

- **The Round dropdown on the Pairings tab opens on that same round.** It
  used to open a round robin on round 1 and stay there after round 1 had
  been played. Whatever you pick by hand is still what prints.

### Saving

- **Rename (Save As) offers the next numbered name.** Renaming
  `MCC - 2026-09` now suggests `MCC - 2026-09 - 001`, then `- 002`, then
  `- 003`, so keeping a numbered series through the day is Save As and
  Enter — no typing unless you want to. Anything you added of your own is
  kept and numbered from there: `MCC - 2026-09 - Mark - 001`, `- 002`,
  and so on.

- **The number comes from the folder, not from the file you have open.**
  Reopening an earlier snapshot and saving from it continues the series
  rather than offering a name that already exists — so you are not warned
  about overwriting the file you just saved.

- **A year is left alone.** Only a three-digit ending is treated as a
  sequence, so an event named `MCC - 2026` becomes `MCC - 2026 - 001`
  rather than being quietly advanced to `MCC - 2027`.

### Sections list

- **A collapsed section row keeps its numbers.** Collapsing a row used to
  hide the player and round counts — the very things a collapsed list is
  scanned for. The name now carries them: `Open [123, 3/9]` is 123
  players, 3 rounds played of 9.

## Installer

Windows x64. The installer bundles the .NET runtime, so there is nothing
to install first. Run it and FreePair updates in place, keeping your
settings and recent-events list.

## Known limitations

**Platforms.** This release is Windows only. macOS and Linux are still on
`v0.90.20260829` and will be brought level on the next full release.

**Quads.** The one-page sheet is for quads specifically — sections
FreePair formed with **Split into Quads**. A four-player round robin you
built by hand keeps printing a page per round, deliberately: changing how
an existing event prints because of its size would be a surprise rather
than a convenience.

**Pairing fidelity.** FreePair's USCF engine is validated against a large
real-world SwissSys corpus and reproduces the great majority of pairings
exactly, reaching 100% on many representative events. The remaining
differences are in areas where the rules leave the director discretion —
board ordering within a score group, and which player takes a bye when
several are equally eligible — so both answers are legal.
