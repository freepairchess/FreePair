# FreePair v0.95.20260901

**All four platforms.** Windows, macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) and
Linux are level again on this release — macOS and Linux were last built
at `v0.90.20260829`, so this one carries everything from `v0.91` through
`v0.95` for them.

This release is about pairing a multi-section event in one go, and about
quads.

## What's new

### Pair all sections

- **The dialog stays open and shows you the run.** It used to close the
  moment you pressed Pair, leaving the event to be paired behind an
  unchanged window — several seconds per section with nothing on screen
  to say so, and no way to tell how far it had got. It now stays up with
  a progress bar, *Section 3 of 5 — Open*, and a line naming what is
  happening right now: checking player IDs, calculating pairings, saving
  the round. Each section's row reports the round it paired, or why it
  did not.

- **Stop after this section** halts a run cleanly. The section being
  paired finishes and the rest are left alone; rounds already committed
  stay committed.

- **Choose which sections pair.** Every ready section starts ticked, so
  the default is still "pair everything" — untick the one waiting on a
  player who has not arrived. The **Pair?** heading ticks or clears them
  all.

- **One click really does pair everything now.** The questions FreePair
  used to ask once per section are answered once, at the top of the
  dialog: whether to review each round in the pairing preview (off by
  default), the round 1 colour for every section that has not paired yet
  (a coin toss per section by default), and whether to re-seed each
  roster first. Five sections used to mean five colour prompts and five
  previews between the click and the finished event.

- **Re-seeding follows each section's own rules.** A FIDE section ranks
  title before name; a US Chess section goes straight to name. An event
  mixing engines or rating scales still gets the right order in each
  section.

- **Nothing starts without a confirmation.** Pressing **Pair ready
  sections** first lists every section and what it will get — *pairs
  round 1*, *pairs round 3* — and says whether rounds will be previewed
  or committed straight through.

- **Sections open on the board numbers they should have.** A section you
  have never placed now starts at its recommended board rather than at 1,
  so the sections stack end to end instead of every one of them claiming
  board 1.

- **The table reads like a table**, with aligned columns and grid lines,
  and carries the section's pairing rule, starting board, board range,
  pairing engine, teammate avoidance and acceleration. The pairing rule
  is editable there, and a change you make is saved to the section — set
  a section to Quad, close the dialog, and pairing it later from its own
  tab still pairs it as quads.

### Quads

- **Teammates are kept out of the same quad.** A quad is a four-player
  round robin, so two teammates in one are certain to play each other and
  no later pairing decision can prevent it. With **Avoid same team** on,
  FreePair separates them while it is cutting the field up, moving as few
  players out of rating order as it can. Players who end up in the
  leftover mini-Swiss are not moved — there, avoiding teammates is an
  ordinary pairing constraint.

- **A team too large to separate is handled predictably.** When a team
  has more players than there are groups, somebody has to share; the
  strongest players are separated first and what remains is left at the
  bottom of the field, where it affects the fewest results.

- **Every split says what it did** — how many quads, whether there is a
  mini-Swiss, and what happened to the teammate separation: nothing
  needed doing, done by moving *N* players, or not fully possible and
  why. A section of exactly four players is one quad and cannot separate
  anybody, and now says so instead of appearing to succeed.

- **The leftover mini-Swiss is paired too**, its first round, as part of
  the same operation. It used to be left ready and empty, which is easy
  to walk away from.

- **A quad section stops offering settings it cannot use.** Acceleration
  is not shown at all, and teammate avoidance is labelled *Keep teammates
  in different quads* with no round count — a quad is three rounds long
  and everybody meets everybody, so a round window would restate the
  event. A four-player quad shows neither.

## Installer

Windows x64, macOS (Apple silicon and Intel) and Linux x64. The installer
bundles the .NET runtime, so there is nothing to install first. Run it
and FreePair updates in place, keeping your settings and recent-events
list.

## Known limitations

**Round robins and quads have no pairing preview.** Their schedule is
computed rather than judged, so there is nothing to accept — the
pairings appear as soon as they are generated.

**Phone formatting is North American.** Seven, ten and eleven digit
numbers are regrouped for reading; anything else is shown exactly as it
was typed.

**Pairing fidelity.** FreePair's USCF engine is validated against a large
real-world SwissSys corpus and reproduces the great majority of pairings
exactly, reaching 100% on many representative events. The remaining
differences are in areas where the rules leave the director discretion —
board ordering within a score group, and which player takes a bye when
several are equally eligible — so both answers are legal.
