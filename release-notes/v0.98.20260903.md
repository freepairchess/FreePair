# FreePair v0.98.20260903

## What's new

### Ratings now know which month your event is played on

**A rating is not a fact about a player — it is a fact about a player on
a supplement.** Until this release, "refresh ratings" was treated as
unambiguously helpful. It is not. An event that starts on 30 August is
played on August's ratings all the way through, including the rounds you
pair in September; refreshing it then would re-seed a running event on a
list it is not being played under, and the standings would stop agreeing
with the seeding.

FreePair now works out which supplement your event is played on **from
its start date**, never from today's, and says so before it does
anything.

**Round 1 will not pair without a start date.** It is the one piece of
information that decides which ratings the field is seeded on, so
guessing it is not an option. FreePair asks for it and explains why.

**A review before any rating changes.** Refreshing ratings — whether
from the Roster tab or from the check before pairing — now shows what it
is about to do: your event, its start date, the supplement that follows
from it, today's date, and a table of every player whose roster rating
differs from the supplement's, with the difference and a recommendation
for each. **The recommendation can be "do not refresh"**, and when it is,
the button becomes *Refresh anyway* behind a tick. Whichever action is
the wrong one that day is the one you have to acknowledge.

**When the rating database has not published yet, FreePair says so and
tells you to wait.** A supplement is published on the 1st, but the
services that serve it do not all take it in that morning. Ask for
September's ratings on 1 September and you may be handed August's — with
no error, because as far as the database is concerned it answered your
question. Press refresh, watch numbers change, and walk away believing
your roster holds the current ratings when it holds last month's.

That is worse than not refreshing at all, because it looks like it
worked. FreePair reads the rating list date that comes back with every
lookup, names the month the database actually served, and recommends
waiting rather than refreshing.

### Knowing when your roster disagrees with the site

**A red "!" appears on any section holding a player whose rating differs
from the one they are registered with on NA Chess Hub.** Not a lookup in
the national rating database — the event's own roster on the site, which
is the number the player reads beside their own name and the one they
will ask you about. Click it for the list, with a **Section** column and
the option to take the site's numbers or to ignore the difference for
this event.

It rides on the roster check FreePair already makes, so it costs no extra
network calls, and it needs the event to have NA Chess Hub credentials.

**A separate, optional check compares your roster with the published
national rating before each pairing.** These are genuinely different
questions and both are now settings on the event's **NA Chess Hub** tab
(previously called Results Publishing).

### Board numbering, tight or loose

**FreePair now offers SwissSys's board packing choice.** *Loose* — the
default and the safe one — reserves each section enough boards for every
player in it. *Tight* reserves only enough for the players actually being
paired next round, so a section holding byes needs fewer boards and the
next section starts sooner, giving an event boards numbered 1 to N with
no empty tables. *Prompt* asks you for each section's starting board as
it is paired.

**A round can now be renumbered without re-pairing it.** Both from the
event's Renumber Boards dialog, which has a button per section, and from
a section's own Pairing Operations menu. Each asks for the starting board
and offers the recommended one.

The choice is **per event, with your own default for new events** under
**⚙ Settings → Boards**. Changing your default does not reach back into
events you have already set up.

### Smaller things

**The colour-due legend is only printed when it explains something.**
Pairing sheets carried a legend for colour codes even when no colour
column was on the page, and on round 1 — where nobody has a colour
history yet — it could push a two-page sheet onto a third.

**The event card shows the organising club's logo.** An event linked to
NA Chess Hub now carries a badge: the club's own logo when they have
uploaded one, otherwise the NA Chess Hub logo. Click it to open that
event's roster page on the site.

**Choose the sound FreePair alerts you with**, under Settings → Online,
with a Test button.

## Installer

Windows x64. The installer bundles the .NET runtime, the pairing engines
and the user guide — nothing else to install. Run it and FreePair
updates itself in place; your events and settings are untouched.

## Known limitations

- **Windows only in this release.** macOS and Linux stay on the previous
  version and will catch up on the next full run.
- **The rating checks need the event to be linked to NA Chess Hub.** The
  red "!" compares against that event's roster on the site, so an event
  with no NA Chess Hub id has nothing to compare against.
- **If the rating database cannot be reached, pairing goes ahead.** An
  outage must never be able to stop a round going out, so a failed check
  is treated as though the check were switched off rather than as a
  reason to refuse.
- **Ratings are never changed for you.** Every difference FreePair finds
  is reported; applying it is always your decision.
- **Tight board packing can move a section's range between rounds** as
  bye counts change. This is inherent to packing tightly, which is why
  Loose remains the default.
- **The organising club's logo is a convenience, not a guarantee.** It is
  read from the event's page on NA Chess Hub; if it cannot be fetched,
  the badge shows the NA Chess Hub logo instead and nothing is reported.
