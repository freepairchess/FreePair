# FreePair v0.83.20260825

**Windows only.** If you are on Linux or macOS, stay on
**v0.81.20260823** — this release has no build for you, and your update
check will have nothing to find until the next one that does.

This release is about FIDE norms: working out who has earned one, telling
a player why they have not, and building an event from a published entry
list so the roster is right before round one.

## What's new

### FIDE norms

- **A Norms tab for FIDE-rated sections.** It answers the three questions
  a director actually gets asked: does this event support norms at all,
  is it a "large Swiss" under Article 1.4.3d, and where does each player
  stand. Everything on it is read-only — nothing there can change your
  event.
- **Every player is checked against every norm they are actually
  chasing.** A grandmaster needs none and is left out. A male
  international master is shown the GM norm only. A female IM is shown GM
  and WGM but not WIM, because she is already past that title. Listing
  all four norms for everybody buried the two or three players who were
  genuinely close.
- **A full breakdown for any player**, by double-clicking their row: the
  score, average opponent rating and performance rating against what the
  title needs; every requirement of Article 1.4 with its verdict; and
  every game that counted, with each opponent's title, federation, rating
  and result. This is the view to open when somebody asks *why*.
- **A printable norm report for one player**, achieved or not. A refused
  norm prints with the failing requirement named and the shortfall spelt
  out — "needed 7/9 against this opposition; scored 5½, 1½ short".
  Explaining a refusal is the commonest reason to need this document.
- **The large-Swiss check shows its working, round by round.** How many
  rated non-host players, how many federations and how many titled
  players were in the field each round, with any round that fell short
  marked in red. Underneath, the players who did not count towards the
  total and the reason for each — because a total nobody can audit is
  not much use when a norm is being claimed on it.
- **Time controls are checked against Article 1.1.3.** Increments count
  towards the two hours: `G/90;+30` reaches exactly 120 minutes and
  qualifies, the same 90 minutes without the increment does not. Where
  the written control cannot be read with confidence, the tab says so
  and leaves the answer to you rather than guessing.
- **A norm certificate you can sign**, with the full calculation, your
  Article 1.1 answers and signature blocks for the chief arbiter and the
  federation's rating officer.
- **A norms reference that works offline** — what each norm requires,
  worked through, per event type.

### Rosters, IDs and ratings

- **Verify IDs now fills in the FIDE half of the roster.** For a
  FIDE-rated section it looks up each player's FIDE ID, rating,
  federation, title and sex, writes them to the roster, and saves them
  with the event. Norm work is impossible without them and typing them in
  by hand for a large open is not realistic.
- **It is fast now.** Lookups go out in batches rather than one player at
  a time — a thousand-player roster resolves in seconds instead of
  minutes — and stay inside the player database's published rate limit.
- **Refresh Ratings is batched the same way**, for both FIDE and NWSRS
  ratings.
- **One failed lookup no longer abandons the rest of the roster.** A
  single unresolvable ID used to stop the run, leaving later players
  untouched with nothing on screen to say so.
- **Player names on the roster are links** to that player's profile in
  the player database.
- **The ID2 and Rating2 columns name their federation in the heading**,
  so a FIDE ID sitting beside a US Chess one is not left to guesswork.

### Building an event from a published entry list

- **Create a new event from an event already published on the web.**
  FreePair reads the entry list and builds the roster for you: US Chess
  rated events, Chess Federation of Canada events, chess-results-style
  entry lists, and four registration sites.
- **Multi-section events come across whole**, with a progress dialog
  while they do.
- **US Chess events bring their own details** — dates, location,
  organiser and chief TD.

### Fixes from real events

- **A withheld player no longer lends their pair number to somebody
  else.** This could corrupt the file sent to the pairing engine.
- **Byes granted mid-event now show on the roster.** They were being
  applied correctly and were invisible, so a director had no way to
  confirm what they had just done.
- **A player who returns after sitting out is no longer shown as
  withdrawn.**
- **FreePair reopens your last event only if it was open when you
  closed it.** Closing an event and then quitting no longer reopens it
  next time.
- **The round's byes are their own table on the Pairings tab**, with a
  focus mode, instead of being folded into the pairings.
- **Several menu fixes** — clipped labels, squashed icons, and menus that
  could open on a separator.

## Installer

The installer bundles everything FreePair needs, including the .NET
runtime and the bbpPairings engine. Nothing else to install.

Download `FreePair-win-x64-Setup.exe` and run it. A portable zip is also
attached for running from a USB stick.

## Known limitations

**Windows only.** Linux and macOS builds are not in this release. TDs on
those platforms should stay on **v0.81.20260823**; an update check may
report a failure until a release with those builds is published.

**Norms are a calculation, not a ruling.** FreePair works out Article 1.4
from your crosstable — games, opponent mix, average rating, performance
rating, the federation rules. It cannot see Article 1.1: whether the
event was registered with FIDE in time, whether the chief arbiter held
the right licence and was in the venue. Those are yours to confirm on the
tab, and **anything you leave unanswered prints as outstanding, never as
a pass**.

**The large-Swiss check is read strictly.** Article 1.4.3d asks for the
field "in every round", and FreePair takes that literally: one round
below a threshold fails the section. A field of exactly twenty has no
slack — a single player taking one permitted bye leaves nineteen at the
board that round. This is the stricter of two defensible readings, chosen
so the risk is telling a player they have no norm rather than validating
one that would later be refused.

**Norm figures depend on the FIDE data being there.** A roster with no
FIDE federations cannot be checked against 1.4.3d at all, and the tab
says so rather than reporting zeroes as a failure. Run Verify IDs first.

**Women's norms need the player's sex on the roster.** Only women can
earn WGM and WIM norms and FreePair will not guess, so those norms are
not assessed for a player whose sex is unrecorded. The tab counts those
players and tells you. Verify IDs fills it in.

**Event files saved by an earlier FreePair may need Verify IDs run
once.** FIDE federation, FIDE rating and sex were not being written to
the file before this release, so they were lost when an event was
reopened. Re-running Verify IDs repairs it permanently.
