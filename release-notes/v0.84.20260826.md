# FreePair v0.84.20260826

**Windows only.** If you are on Linux or macOS, stay on
**v0.81.20260823** — this release has no build for you, and your update
check will have nothing to find until the next one that does.

This release is mostly about pairings. Two directors sent in rounds where
FreePair and SwissSys disagreed, and between them they turned up four
separate faults: requested byes going missing, and colours being handed out
worse than they needed to be. All four are fixed. There is also a faster
way to start an event from one already published on the web.

## What's new

### Byes are honoured, and they stay honoured

- **A bye you asked for is given in the round you asked for it.** A request
  for the round you are pairing right now could be treated as though it had
  already been served, which put players who had asked to sit out back onto
  boards. On the reported event that was nine boards where there should
  have been eight, with two byes silently dropped.
- **Pair, look, unpair, pair again — the byes come back.** This is the
  ordinary way a round gets reviewed, and it is how the fault above was
  found. Requests that were honoured the first time are honoured the second
  time.
- **That now survives closing and reopening the event.** Saving a scored
  event used to quietly delete the record of a zero-point bye that had
  already been taken. Nobody lost a point over it — the round kept the
  result — but reopen the file, unpair that round, and the request was gone
  with nothing to put it back. Half-point requests were never affected.

### Better colours in the bottom brackets

- **Fewer players get the wrong colour when a score group is odd.** When an
  odd group has to send a player down to the next group, which player goes
  decides whether the colours in both groups can come out clean. FreePair
  sent the lowest-rated player down as a matter of course, even when doing
  so guaranteed two players would be handed the same colour twice running.
  It now looks ahead, and will send a different player down when that
  clears the conflict — which is what an experienced director does by hand.
- **A player floated down is no longer pinned to an opponent who wants the
  same colour.** The rule that a floated player meets the strongest
  available opponent now gives way when it is the sole cause of a colour
  clash, within the rating limits the rulebook sets for that kind of swap.

Both changes were checked against a large corpus of real SwissSys events
before shipping, board by board rather than round by round, and neither
costs a single board anywhere else.

### Starting an event from the web

- **NA Chess Hub events can be read from their public pages**, without an
  event passcode. If the entry list is published, FreePair can build your
  roster from it.
- **Scholastic entry lists from progressly.org**, with each player's school
  brought in as their team — so team scoring and team-avoidance work from
  the start rather than after a round of typing.

### Norms tab

- **Rebuilt as three panels** — the event's own eligibility, the players in
  contention, and the projection — each with the filtering, sorting and
  column controls every other table in FreePair has, and its own focus mode
  and Print button.
- **It now says what the last game is worth.** For each player still in
  contention: the result they need in the final round, and — just as
  useful — who is already out of reach, so you are not asked about it at
  the board.
- **An earned norm is marked in green** rather than needing to be read out
  of a number.

### Roster and dialogs

- **A counting column on the roster**, so a filtered or sorted list can
  simply be counted.
- **The FIDE country column appears on a FIDE-rated section inside a US
  Chess event**, instead of only on wholly FIDE events.
- **A paste button on the web event address**, and the roster's copy icons
  are lined up.
- **The round-robin setup dialog is big enough to seed a field in**, and
  seeding can be set by hand.

## Installer

The installer bundles everything FreePair needs, including the .NET
runtime and the bbpPairings engine. Nothing else to install.

Download `FreePair-win-x64-Setup.exe` and run it. A portable zip is also
attached for running from a USB stick.

## Known limitations

**Windows only.** Linux and macOS builds are not in this release. TDs on
those platforms should stay on **v0.81.20260823**; an update check may
report a failure until a release with those builds is published.

**Pairings still differ from SwissSys on a small number of rounds.**
FreePair reproduces the great majority of pairings in a large real-world
corpus of SwissSys events exactly, and reaches 100% on many representative
events. Where the two differ it is almost always a matter the rulebook
leaves to the director's discretion — which of two equally-lawful players
floats down, or which of two tied transpositions is taken — rather than one
side being wrong. Every such case is documented with the reasoning behind
it.

**A requested bye is a request, not a promise.** FreePair honours one
whenever the round allows it, but a bye cannot always be given — the rules
on byes and on an odd field come first.

**Team byes still cannot be entered from the team roster.** Individual byes
are unaffected; this is only the team-level button.

**The Norms tab reports a calculation, not a ruling.** It works out
Article 1.4 from your crosstable. Article 1.1 — registration, arbiter
licensing, presence in the venue — remains yours to confirm, and anything
left unanswered prints as outstanding rather than as a pass.
