# FreePair v0.85.20260826

**Windows only.** If you are on Linux or macOS, stay on
**v0.81.20260823** — this release has no build for you, and your update
check will have nothing to find until the next one that does.

This release is about prizes. FreePair could hold a list of cash amounts
labelled 1st, 2nd and 3rd, and split them between players who tied. That
is one rule out of a chapter. Class prizes, under prizes, top female, top
junior, top unrated, biggest upset, trophies — none of them could be
expressed, so a director running an event with a class prize fund worked
the whole thing out on paper and then had nowhere to record what they had
decided.

All of it is in now, along with the rules that decide who gets what when
somebody qualifies for more than one.

## What's new

### Every kind of prize your event announces

- **Place, class and under prizes.** Add a class or under prize from a
  menu and its rating window is already correct — you never type
  "1600–1799", so you never mistype it.
- **Special prizes:** biggest upset, top female, top junior, top senior,
  top unrated, and top player from a named club or state.
- **Director's awards** — best game, brilliancy, sportsmanship, or
  anything else you want to call it. FreePair lists them and waits for you
  to name the winner, because nothing can compute them.
- **Trophies and medals.** Tick "Trophy or medal — not cash" and the
  prize stops being money: no amount, and because an object cannot be cut
  in half, a tie for it is settled on your section's tiebreaks instead of
  being shared. Winning one does not use up the player's cash prize, so
  the same player can take the champion's trophy and the first-place
  money.

### The rules that decide who gets what

- **A player wins one cash prize — the largest they are eligible for.**
  The player who is top Under 1600 *and* wins the section outright takes
  first place, and the Under 1600 prize passes down to the next eligible
  player. That roll-down is the part most directors are looking for.
- **Players tied on score share.** The prizes they claim between them are
  added together and divided equally. Tiebreaks are never used for cash.
- **Nobody takes more than they qualified for.** In a shared pool, a
  player is capped at the largest single prize they are individually
  eligible for, and what the cap frees up goes to the others.
- **The tie is resolved to pay out as much as possible.** Where a group
  of tied players can reach more prizes than there are players in the
  tie, FreePair picks the combination that distributes the most — the
  calculation a director is asked to do by hand.
- All three rules can be switched off in the editor if your announcement
  said something different. That is the condition the rulebook itself
  puts on them.

**Biggest upset** is worked out from the games: the largest rating gap in
a decisive win by the lower-rated player. Draws do not count, wins by
forfeit do not count, and a game with an unrated player on either side
does not count — there is no rating there to be upset by.

### Knowing before the prize-giving

- **The Prize fund table counts who can actually win each prize.** A
  prize nobody in the section qualifies for is named in a warning above
  the table. This is worth a glance the moment you finish setting prizes
  up: a prize nobody can win looks exactly like a correct one, and
  without this you find out in front of whoever thought they had won it.
- **Not awarded** lists every declared prize that went to nobody, and
  why — no eligible player, fewer players than prizes, no upset played,
  or a director's award still waiting for you.
- **Prize projection**, while rounds remain: what the list looks like on
  the scores so far, and for each contender what they take if they win
  out and if they do not. A player whose worst case is above zero is in
  the money whatever happens, which is the question actually asked at the
  desk before the last round. These are bounds, not predictions —
  FreePair does not calculate odds, because it does not know any.

### Changing an award yourself

Select any prize and press **Edit award…** to name different recipients,
change how the money splits, or withhold it entirely. Your note prints
beside the award. No rulebook decides a best-game prize, a player who has
gone home, or a condition that was in your entry form.

### On the wall and in print

- **The wallboard has a prize screen**, on the projector and on the web
  view both. Until you press Calculate it shows the prize *fund* — each
  prize, who can win it, the amount — and only afterwards the winners. It
  will not name a winner before you have.
- **The printed report** carries the winners, the unawarded prizes with
  their reasons, the fund with its eligibility column, and a footnote
  stating the sharing rules that produced the numbers.

### The Prizes tab

Rebuilt as collapsible panels, each with the Focus button, filter box and
font stepper the other tabs already had. The filters are per panel, so
searching the winners for a player does not quietly narrow the fund you
are reading beside it.

**Also fixed:** pressing F11 on the Norms tab used to leave a blank page.

## Installer

`FreePair-win-Setup.exe` is a single-file installer for Windows x64. It
bundles the .NET runtime and the FIDE pairing engine, so there is nothing
to install first. It adds a Start Menu entry and an uninstaller, and
updates itself from this feed.

## Known limitations

**Prizes**

- **Based-on prize funds are not modelled.** If your announcement says
  "based on 60 entries", work out the prorated amounts yourself and enter
  those. FreePair will not scale a fund to turnout, and does not know
  about the requirement to pay a minimum share of an advertised based-on
  fund.
- **A team section awards place prizes only.** Teams are ranked by match
  points, so a rating band or a date of birth has nothing to attach to.
  Other prizes in a team section are listed and left for you to award.
- **Age and sex come from the roster.** A junior prize needs dates of
  birth, and a female prize needs the Sex column. A player with the field
  blank is not eligible — FreePair will not guess in order to hand out
  money.

**Platforms**

- **Windows only, for the fifth release running.** Linux and macOS builds
  are still to come.

**Files**

- **A file written by this version can be opened by an older FreePair**,
  which will simply ignore the prize detail it does not understand — and
  will drop it if you then edit prizes there. Keep a copy before opening
  a current file in an older build.
