# FreePair v0.88.20260827

**Every platform again.** v0.87 was Windows only; this release has
builds for Windows, Linux and macOS, so update checks work everywhere
once more.

This release is about one thing: if you run events on NA Chess Hub, you
can now sign in to FreePair once and stop hunting for passcodes.

## What's new

### Sign in to NA Chess Hub

**⚙ Settings → Online → NA Chess Hub sign-in.** Sign in once and
FreePair can reach every event your account runs — including events
delegated to you — without a passcode for each one.

Your browser opens on NA Chess Hub's own sign-in page, so **FreePair
never sees your password** and any two-factor step stays between you and
them. Once you are back, FreePair shows which account is connected by
its NA Chess Hub name, such as *PNW Chess Center (PNWCC - Organizer)*,
rather than the email address it was registered with — worth a glance if
you hold both a personal login and a club one.

**Signing in is optional and changes nothing else.** Per-event passcodes
work exactly as before, which is still the right tool when somebody
hands you one event to run for them.

### Two menus for your own events

- **➕ New Event → Create New Event Using Online Registry → My Events on
  NA Chess Hub…** — start a *new* event from its NA Chess Hub entry
  list.
- **📂 Open Event → My Events Saved on NA Chess Hub…** — re-open an
  event you have already been running, with its pairings and results as
  you left them.

If you are in the middle of an event, you want the second one. Each list
remembers its own search text, and each shows only your **current**
events — tick **Show all my events on NA Chess Hub** to include finished
ones, so a club with years of history does not have to scroll past all
of it to find tonight's tournament.

### Switching accounts takes one click

If your browser is already signed in to NA Chess Hub, FreePair signs in
as that account straight away without asking — and signing out of
FreePair and back in gives you the same account again, because NA Chess
Hub remembers you in the *browser*. That surprised people, reasonably.

**Use a different account…** now signs your browser out of NA Chess Hub
and opens their sign-in page so you can pick the account you want. The
signed-out panel also says this up front rather than leaving you to
discover it.

### Uploading still uses the event passcode

Publishing pairings and results, cloud backup and roster sync are
unchanged by signing in — they use the per-event passcode exactly as
they always have. This is deliberate: an upload works or fails for the
same reason every time, whether or not you happen to be signed in.
Nothing is lost by it, because an event you downloaded by signing in
brings its passcode with it.

### Creating an event from a web address needs a sign-in

**Create New Web Event** now requires an NA Chess Hub account. The
dialog offers the sign-in itself, and if you do not have an account
there is a **Create a free account** button beside it — an account at
nachesshub.com costs nothing and takes about a minute.

## Installer

`FreePair-win-x64-Setup.exe` is a single-file installer for Windows x64,
with Linux and macOS builds attached alongside. It bundles the .NET
runtime and the FIDE pairing engine, so there is nothing to install
first. It adds a Start Menu entry and an uninstaller, and updates itself
from this feed. A portable zip is attached for anyone who prefers not to
install.

## Known limitations

- **Signing out of FreePair does not cancel your sign-in at NA Chess
  Hub.** It removes it from that computer; it expires on their side on
  its own within two weeks. There is no way to revoke it from FreePair
  yet. On a shared or borrowed machine, sign out when you are done.
- **You may be asked to sign in again after a few weeks.** The sign-in
  lasts about a fortnight, so returning to a tournament after time away
  is the usual reason.
- **"Show all my events" depends on NA Chess Hub.** The option is sent
  with the request; if their service does not yet act on it, the list is
  unchanged rather than wrong.
- **Pairing fidelity.** FreePair's USCF pairings are validated against a
  large corpus of real events and reproduce the great majority of them.
  Accelerated pairings, team events and director intervention are areas
  where a published event and a re-pairing can legitimately differ.
