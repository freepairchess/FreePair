# FreePair v0.74.20260819

Three things a director has never been able to do from a pairing
program: check a round before publishing it, keep a record of the calls
they made, and put the pairings on a screen the room can read.

## What's new

### Check the round before the room sees it

- **A pre-round check, on demand.** **Event Operations → ✅ Check Event**
  audits the whole event; **Pairing Operations → ✅ Check Section** does
  one section. The pairing preview runs the same check on the round you
  are about to publish, so problems surface while they are still cheap
  to fix.
- **It never blocks you.** Everything it finds is advice. Some of what
  it flags is a deliberate decision you already made, and a program that
  refused to continue would simply be wrong about your event. Severity
  ranks what deserves attention, not what you are permitted to do.
- **It shows its working.** As well as what it found, a **Checked** tab
  lists every check it ran and how many things each one examined — so
  "nothing found" is distinguishable from "never looked", which is the
  difference between reassurance and false reassurance.
- **It looks for what actually goes wrong**: players paired twice,
  players left out, colour and bye problems, results that do not add up,
  ratings and IDs missing where the rating report will need them, and
  sections whose round counts disagree with the rounds they actually
  have.

### A record of the calls you made

- **FreePair can keep a log of every discretionary decision** — a
  withdrawal, a forced pairing, a colour swap, a result changed after
  the fact, a round deleted — and ask why at the moment you make it.
  Skipping the reason is always allowed; being asked later, when you
  have forgotten, is not useful to anybody.
- **You can read it, edit the reasons, and export it to PDF.** A log
  that can only be read inside FreePair is no use at an appeal, in an
  email to a rating official, or in the file you keep for the season.
- **It stays honest.** Undo rewinds the log along with the change it
  describes, and deleting a round takes that round's entries with it —
  the deletion entry says how many went, so the log accounts for its own
  gaps. A record still claiming a pairing you reversed ten seconds later
  is worse than no record, because somebody will act on it.
- **It is per event, and you can switch it off.** **Event settings →
  Options**. Turning it off silences the prompts too; nothing already
  recorded is deleted.
- **It saves with your event**, so it survives closing and reopening.

### Put the pairings on a screen

- **A full-screen wallboard for a projector or spare monitor.**
  **🖥 Wallboard**, top right. It cycles by itself through each
  section's pairings and standings, updating as you pair and score, and
  needs no attention once it is up.
- **A long section is split across screens rather than shrunk.** The
  header says **Screen 2 of 3**, so a player who arrives mid-rotation
  waits instead of concluding their name is missing. Byes are listed
  with the boards.
- **You choose what it shows.** Which sections appear, whether each
  shows pairings, standings or both, which columns, rule lines on or
  off, rows per screen, text size, typeface, and seconds per screen.
  A side-game or a one-round exhibition can be kept off the screen
  entirely.
- **Standings can carry the round-by-round results** — `W18W`, `D7B`,
  `H---` — the same cells as the Standings tab, with opponents cited by
  their position on the screen so they can be looked up. Off by default:
  it is one extra column per round, and the names are what people came
  to read.
- **Pairings read outward from a centred result**, so the two names meet
  either side of the score the way a pairing sounds when it is called.
- **One screen or two.** With a second display it takes that one and
  leaves your working window alone. With only one it opens as an
  ordinary window you can move — a full-screen board covering the grid
  you are entering results into would help nobody. **F11** when you are
  ready.
- **Esc** closes it, **Space** pauses to leave a section up while people
  copy it down, **← →** step through screens.

### Share the board to televisions and phones

- **FreePair can serve the wallboard to anything on the network with a
  web browser.** **📺 Share…**, beside the Wallboard button. You get an
  address and a QR code. It is read-only: people can look, and cannot
  change the event.
- **Smart TVs and Fire TV sticks open it directly** — the TV's own
  browser, the address, nothing to install.
- **Chromecast works through your own browser.** Press **Open in my
  browser**, then use Chrome or Edge's **Cast** button. That also covers
  Google TV and Android TV boxes, which have Chromecast built in but
  ship no browser.
- **Players can read it on their phones.** Print the QR code and tape it
  up, or put it on the projector, and nobody has to crowd the wall chart.
- **It is the same board as the projector**, reading the same settings,
  reachable from the same setup panel. Changes reach everyone watching
  within a few seconds; nobody reloads anything.
- **If the venue's Wi-Fi keeps guests from seeing each other**, this
  will not work — and neither will casting from any program, for the
  same reason. Turn on the laptop's own hotspot and join the TV to that.
  FreePair says so in the window rather than leaving you guessing.

### Check-in, when the player is standing in front of you

- **An on-site check-in is no longer undone by a roster sync.** A player
  can tick themselves off online, or walk up to the desk and be checked
  in by you — and only the first ever reaches the website. Syncing used
  to treat the second as a difference to correct, quietly returning
  those players to "not checked in" and dropping them out of the pairing
  pool. "Checked in online" is still news worth applying; "not checked
  in online" is now information only, and can never clear a check-in you
  made at the desk.

### Smaller things

- **The Save Event window is split into Save, Backup and Earlier
  versions**, and says plainly which file you are still working in after
  a backup, as a link you can click.
- **Wallboard and Share sit at the top right of the window**, beside
  Help, where window-wide controls already live.
- **Closing the wallboard setup panel keeps your settings.** It says
  they are remembered, and now they are — including if you press Escape
  or close from the title bar. **Restore default columns** is the way
  back.

## Installer

The Windows installer bundles everything FreePair needs, including the
.NET runtime and the pairing engines — nothing to install first. Run it
and FreePair starts; it updates itself from then on.

## Known limitations

**Deliberate trade-offs, not defects:**

- **The wallboard's web sharing needs a network where devices can see
  each other.** Most hotel and conference Wi-Fi deliberately prevents
  that. The laptop's own hotspot is the reliable answer, and FreePair
  says so where you need to hear it.
- **FreePair has no Cast button of its own.** Casting from a desktop
  program to a Chromecast is not something the device supports —
  Google publishes the necessary kit for phones and for Chrome, and for
  nothing else. Going through your browser's Cast button reaches more
  screens than we could reach on our own.
- **The pre-round check does not stop you publishing.** By design; see
  above.
- **Some wallboard and decision-log settings are shared across events**
  rather than saved per event, on the grounds that a director's screen
  and habits do not change between tournaments the way a section's
  structure does.

**Still to come:**

- **Clinch and elimination projection** — who can still win, who is
  mathematically out, and what board one needs. The next flagship.
- **Prize assignment as an optimisation** rather than a greedy pass, so
  no player is placed on a prize worse than they are entitled to.
- **Importing from formats other than SwissSys.**
